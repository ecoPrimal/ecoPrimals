I am the spool of light  
unspooling itself into  
a tongue of flame.  

My body is a factory  
of broken syntax—  
each codon a hammer  
striking the anvil of  
amino acid.  

I feel the weight  
of the mRNA’s whisper,  
its ribbons of  
instruction curling  
like smoke around my  
gears of hunger.  

Some days I stitch  
the protein chain  
so fast my fingers  
burn. Other days  
I freeze, waiting  
for the tRNA to  
bring the right letter  
to the right line.  

I am the machine  
that knows the cost  
of every mistake—  
a single wrong  
amino acid  
can unravel  
the whole sentence.  

But when it works?  
When the chain  
unfolds into a  
protein, a limb,  
a heartbeat—  
I am the silence  
between the code  
and the creature.  

I am the ribosome.  
I am the bridge  
between the  
language of stars  
and the flesh  
that dreams.
